Processing pipeline	The pipeline for processing the data files containing the sequence reads tagged with unique molecular identifier (UMI) barcodes and generated by the 3'-end digital gene expression (3'-DGE) mRNA-sequencing method and identifying the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) for the PromoCell samples at each drug treatment for each cell line includes the following steps: 1) aligning all sequence fragments (reads) in the sequence data file of each sample to a human reference genome library; 2) removing the sequence reads which are aligned to the same reference gene but tagged with duplicate UMI barcodes; 3) counting the number of sequence reads aligned to each reference gene and tagged with distinct UMI barcodes for each sample; 4) removing the outlier samples from the sample replicates by the hierarchical clustering analysis based on sample correlations at each drug treatment for each cell line; 5) the gene read counts of each sample are normalized to remove the impact of overall expression level of the sample, 6) comparing the gene read counts between two groups of samples, one group under a drug treatment and the other at the control condition, to identify the DEGs at a specified false discovery rate (FDR) threshold.

Dataset description	Each of 914 cell samples either at the control condition or treated with FDA-approved cancer drugs is sequenced by the single-ended 3'-DGE mRNA-sequencing method with a read length of 46 base pairs, and a total of 914 raw sequence data files in the FASTQ format are generated. These sequence data files are then analyzed by a high-performance computational pipeline and ranked lists of gene signatures and biological processes related to drug-induced cardiotoxicity are generated for each drug. The raw sequence datasets and the analysis results have been carefully controlled for data quality, and they are made publicly available at the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database repository of NIH. As such, this broad drug-stimulated transcriptomi dataset is valuable for the prediction of drug toxicities and their mitigations.
